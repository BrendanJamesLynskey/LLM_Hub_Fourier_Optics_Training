Generated by `analysis/train_flop_share.py` on 2026-10-12 (x86_64, Python 3.13.16, NumPy 2.5.3). Every number in the FOptTrn decks comes from this file.

Conventions: as the inference series (matmul 2mnk; real FFT 2.5 n log2 n; complex pointwise multiply 6, add 2; causal long convolutions zero-padded to a power of two at least 2L). Backward of a matmul is 2x its forward. Softmax, norms, activations, activation recomputation and AdamW's element-wise work are not counted. Shape: Llama-3-8B. An optimizer step is 1,048,576 tokens (illustrative).

## 3. The optimizer step and DeMo

DeMo transforms the momentum in chunks of 64 with a DCT and sends only the largest coefficients. Counting a DCT and an inverse DCT per chunk at the real-FFT cost:

| Parameters | DeMo transform FLOPs per step | Step FLOPs (transformer, 4,096) | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8,029,995,008 | 2.409e+11 | 5.059e+16 | 4.76e-06 |

The optimizer's transforms are negligible in FLOPs; their value is in bytes not sent, which is the Compute in Transit series' subject, not a compute engine's.

## 4. Holding the masks: SLM frames per optimizer step (illustrative)

Device as the inference series: 2-megapixel DMD at 1,031 Hz (8-bit), one complex value per pixel (optimistic). Layer-major order: every sequence in the step passes through one layer's mask before it is rewritten. Weight spectra change every step (an inference engine writes them once); the weight gradient's conj(U) is a new mask for every sequence.

*Digital step time* = step FLOPs on an illustrative 64 GPUs at 400 TFLOP/s sustained each. Rewriting the weight masks every step costs time comparable to a whole digital step or more; the data-by-data products would take minutes of mask writes per step, so they stay digital, and only the weight-masked part is a candidate.

## 5. Precision: what a training format asks of the ADC

ENOB needed so the analogue read-out adds no more error than the number format's own rounding (rule: rel = crest x 2^-ENOB x 2/sqrt(12)), on a 4,096-sample long convolution and its gradients. Gradients carry rare spikes (1% of samples x4 here), which raise the crest factor.
